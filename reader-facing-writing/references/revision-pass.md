# Revise before done

Read this on the closing pass, once a full draft exists. Every rule here is a sweep
over finished material — none of them apply while the draft is still being written.

- Re-read as the target reader, reading it aloud to catch stiff or unnatural phrasing; for high-stakes material, run separate passes each from one named viewpoint — a fresh audience seeing it for the first time, the decision-maker, an adversarial expert — and let a claim survive only if it withstands that viewpoint's follow-up question. These are author-side simulations, not evidence that a human understood the artifact; when the stakes justify it, ask a representative reader to paraphrase or execute the material without coaching, and otherwise report that human comprehension was not validated. When asked whether comprehension or execution has been proved, answer in two parts: what remains unvalidated, with a recommendation for an uncoached test by a representative reader when feasible; then the five author-side checks that still reduce risk now — entry point, findability, stop, recovery, and done. Do not drop the second part because the narrow question can be answered without it.
- Test the entry point by hiding the rest: after the first screen or opening block, can a reader accurately paraphrase the conclusion, current state, or first safe action and tell where the supporting detail lives? Fix the hierarchy or opening if the answer depends on reading ahead.
- Test findability with one real lookup question from the artifact's job. A reader should reach the named fact, owner, command, or decision through the headings, labels, and grouping without scanning every paragraph.
- Test actionability for a doing artifact: the operator can identify when to start, what to do, when to stop, how to recover, and what proves completion. Missing operational detail stays in the main path; it is not demoted for visual neatness.
- Remove each visual in turn and ask what relationship becomes harder to perceive. If nothing does, delete or simplify it; if the visual is necessary, confirm its title states the takeaway and its surrounding prose does not duplicate it.
- Cut redundancy, verify facts, and confirm each section opens in the form its job needs: a takeaway for deciding or understanding, an action or outcome for doing, and a searchable term for lookup.
- Delete until the main path fits the ceiling its skeleton in `SKILL.md` sets (one page for a brief or review, twelve steps for a how-to, one paragraph for a notice); what does not fit is demoted to the detail layer, not squeezed into smaller sentences. Make one revision pass that only deletes: drop every sentence whose removal loses no information (a licensed preview is not such a sentence), and shorten every sentence that needs a second read by re-applying the plain-prose cuts sentence by sentence — real action to a concrete subject and active verb, wind-up cut. A first draft usually shrinks substantially this way with nothing missed — verbosity is a drafting artifact, not thoroughness, and the reader pays for it in effort.
- Check that every section, element, and number visibly connects to the document's purpose and to the part before it — one that ties to neither is misplaced or filler and should go.
- When you change a value that recurs — a name, number, date, owner, or cross-reference — search the whole document for every occurrence and stale pointer (counts, "task N", "see above/below", section labels) before declaring done.
- Treat a defect the reader flags as one sample of a class, not a single instance: sweep the whole artifact for that class, fix every occurrence in the same pass, and report how many you found. Fixing only the cited instance conscripts the reader as your reviewer, and they will notice.
- When a revision deletes, softens, or renames something, sweep for everything that depended on it — later callbacks ("as we saw with X"), labels, badges, counts, and structural pointers — because removing an antecedent turns every dependent line into a non-sequitur that its own author can no longer see.
- When a deliverable exists as mirrored artifacts — a script and its rendered deck, an outline and its output, a spec and its implementation — apply every content change to all of them in one pass and re-read the others before declaring done; a stale mirror actively misleads its next reader.
- Cross-reference by name ("see the section on X"), never by position ("see page 12", "as in the previous part"), while the structure can still change — positional pointers go wrong silently on the next reorder.
